## Facts (approximate, as narrated)
- Air is about 21% oxygen, about 280 mg of O₂ per litre. Well-oxygenated water holds only about 8–10 mg/L, roughly 30 times less.
- Water is about 1,000 kg/m³ and air about 1.2 kg/m³, so water is about 800 times denser. Moving it in and out of the lungs would take a lot of work.
- Fish ventilate their gills with a one-way flow: water enters through the mouth and leaves through the gill openings (with counter-current exchange in the gills).
- Liquid breathing: in the 1960s (Kylstra; Clark & Gollan, 1966) mice survived breathing oxygenated liquids, including perfluorocarbons, which dissolve a lot of oxygen.
- Partial liquid ventilation with perfluorocarbon (perflubron) was tested in the 1990s in premature infants with severe respiratory distress.
- Pressure in water increases by about 1 atmosphere for every 10 m of depth. Deep water is also cold.
