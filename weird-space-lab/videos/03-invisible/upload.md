## Facts (approximate, as narrated)
- We see objects because they reflect, scatter or absorb light. To be truly invisible, a body would have to let light pass through unchanged: not absorbed, not scattered, and not bent (its refractive index would have to match the air's).
- Vision works when photoreceptors in the retina (rods and cones) absorb light: the photopigments change shape when they absorb a photon. Light that passes straight through can't be detected, so a fully transparent eye would be blind.
- If the eyes stayed visible (light-absorbing), they would be seen.
- Real "cloaking" devices made from metamaterials or optical arrangements bend light around an object. So far they work only over narrow wavelength ranges (for example microwaves or one band of light), from limited angles, or for small objects.
