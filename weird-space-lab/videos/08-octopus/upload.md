## Facts (approximate, as narrated)
- Octopuses have three hearts. Two branchial (gill) hearts pump blood through the gills, and one systemic heart pumps the oxygenated blood around the body.
- The systemic heart stops beating while an octopus swims (jet propulsion). This is thought to be one reason they tire quickly when swimming and usually crawl.
- Octopus blood carries oxygen with hemocyanin, a copper-based protein that turns blue when it binds oxygen. Human blood uses hemoglobin, which is iron-based and red.
- Hemocyanin works well in cold water with little oxygen, where many octopuses live.
- An octopus has about 500 million neurons, and about two thirds of them are in its arms. The suckers have chemoreceptors (they "taste" by touch), and an arm can respond to things without input from the central brain.
