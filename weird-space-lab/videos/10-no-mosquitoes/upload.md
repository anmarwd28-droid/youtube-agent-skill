## Facts (approximate, as narrated)
- WHO World Malaria Report 2024: about 263 million malaria cases and about 597,000 deaths in 2023. Mosquitoes are the deadliest animals for humans.
- Mosquitoes also spread dengue (WHO estimates up to around 100–400 million infections a year), Zika, yellow fever, chikungunya and other diseases.
- There are more than 3,500 known mosquito species. Only a small fraction bite humans and spread disease (mainly some Anopheles, Aedes and Culex species).
- Mosquito larvae are food for fish, frogs and other animals, and some adult mosquitoes feed on nectar and pollinate plants (for example some orchids).
- Many ecologists (e.g. Fang, Nature 2010) think most ecosystems would adapt if mosquitoes vanished, with other insects filling their roles, though some food webs (like Arctic ones) would change.
- Researchers are testing targeted methods (Wolbachia bacteria, sterile males, gene drives) against only the disease-carrying species.
