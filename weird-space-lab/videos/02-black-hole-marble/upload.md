## Facts (approximate, as narrated)
- Schwarzschild radius r = 2GM/c². For Earth's mass (5.97 × 10²⁴ kg), r ≈ 8.9 mm, so the black hole would be about 1.8 cm across: the size of a marble, and smaller than many coins (for example, a US quarter is 2.43 cm).
- Pull at 1 m: GM/r² ≈ 3.99 × 10¹⁴ m/s². That is about 4 × 10¹³ times Earth's surface gravity (9.81 m/s²), so about 40 trillion.
- Time for something 1 m away to fall in: about (π/2)·√(r³/2GM) ≈ 6 × 10⁻⁸ s. A blink takes about 0.1–0.4 s.
- Matter falling into a black hole is compressed and heated and gives off huge amounts of light and X-rays. Even capped at the Eddington limit (about 4 × 10²⁵ W for this mass), the light reaching anyone on Earth would be far brighter than sunlight (about 1.4 kW/m²).
- Newton's third law: the black hole pulls on Earth exactly as hard as Earth pulls on it. They have the same mass, so the planet falls toward it as much as it falls toward the planet.
- Material torn from the planet would collect in a hot accretion disk around the black hole. How long the whole process would take is uncertain (this is a thought experiment).
