## Facts (approximate, as narrated)
- Assume a rocky sphere 10 m across (radius 5 m) with Earth's average density (~5,500 kg/m³). Its mass is about 2.9 million kg.
- Surface gravity g = GM/r² ≈ 7.7 × 10⁻⁶ m/s², about 1.3 million times weaker than Earth's 9.81 m/s² ("about a million times").
- Escape velocity v = √(2GM/r) ≈ 0.009 m/s, so about 0.9 cm/s. Ants typically walk at a few centimetres per second.
- A normal walking step (~1 m/s) is about 100 times the escape velocity.
- Such a tiny body can't hold any atmosphere: gas molecules move hundreds of metres per second, far above escape velocity.
- Real small asteroids have very weak gravity. ESA's Philae lander bounced on comet 67P in 2014 when its harpoons failed, and NASA's OSIRIS-REx used a quick "touch-and-go" grab to sample asteroid Bennu.
