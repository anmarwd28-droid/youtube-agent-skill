## Facts (approximate, as narrated)
- World population is about 8.1 billion. At an average mass of about 50 kg (children included), that is roughly 4 × 10¹¹ kg, or about 400 million tonnes.
- Earth's mass is 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg, about 1.5 × 10¹³ (15 trillion) times larger.
- The total momentum stays the same, so as we move up by about 0.5 m, Earth moves the other way by about 0.5 m ÷ 1.5 × 10¹³ ≈ 3 × 10⁻¹⁴ m. An atom is about 10⁻¹⁰ m across.
- When we land, Earth moves back by the same amount. Nothing is felt.
- Big crowds jumping together do register on nearby seismometers as small local signals (for example, stadium crowds). People spread across the planet would not add up to a big quake.
- People live in about 24 time zones, so "the same second" falls at every hour of the day somewhere.
