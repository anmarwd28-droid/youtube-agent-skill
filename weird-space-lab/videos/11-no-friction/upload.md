## Facts (approximate, as narrated)
- Walking relies on static friction between the sole and the ground: your foot pushes backwards, and friction pushes you forwards. With no friction your feet slide out (like on perfectly smooth ice, only worse).
- Cars steer, speed up and brake through tyre–road friction. With no friction the wheels can spin or lock, but the car keeps sliding in a straight line.
- Knots hold because of friction between the strands. Nails are held in wood by friction along their shank (and screws by friction on their threads).
- Woven and knitted fabrics and spun yarn hold together through friction between fibres and threads.
- On a slope, static friction is what keeps objects in place. Without it, gravity pulls them downhill.
- When friction returns, sliding objects decelerate quickly, turning their motion into heat and noise (screeching).
