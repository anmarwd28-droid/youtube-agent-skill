## Facts (approximate, as narrated)
- Earth turns once in about 23.93 h, and its equator is about 40,075 km around, so the ground at the equator moves east at about 1,670 km/h. The speed falls toward the poles and is almost zero at the pole itself.
- Momentum is conserved: an object keeps its velocity unless a force changes it. Someone moved instantly from the equator to the pole would keep moving at about 1,670 km/h relative to the ground there.
- A typical 9 mm pistol bullet leaves the barrel at about 350–380 m/s (about 1,260–1,370 km/h). 1,670 km/h is about 464 m/s.
- At the summit of Everest (8,849 m), air pressure is about a third of sea-level pressure, so each breath holds about a third as much oxygen. Fast pressure changes make your ears pop.
