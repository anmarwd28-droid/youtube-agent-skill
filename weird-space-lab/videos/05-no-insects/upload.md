## Facts (approximate, as narrated)
- Klein et al. (2007): 87 of the 115 leading global food crops (about 75%) depend at least partly on animal pollination, mostly by insects.
- Apples and almonds rely heavily on bee pollination, cocoa is pollinated mainly by tiny midges, and coffee yields rise with insect pollination.
- The staple cereals (wheat, rice and maize/corn) are wind-pollinated or self-pollinated, so they would not need insects.
- Many birds, frogs, bats and freshwater fish eat insects. Losing insects would collapse their food supply.
- Insects (dung beetles, flies, termites, beetles) help break down dead plants, dung and carcasses and help recycle nutrients into soil. Fungi and bacteria would keep working, but decomposition would slow down and soil would get poorer.
