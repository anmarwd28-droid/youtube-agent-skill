## Facts (approximate, as narrated)

**Weight and drifting up**
- Weight is the pull of gravity. With no gravity, you weigh nothing, and the ground no longer pushes up on you.
- Earth's equator is about 40,075 km around and Earth turns once in about 23.93 hours, so the ground at the equator moves at about 1,670 km/h (about 465 m/s). Toward the poles this speed falls to zero.
- An object with no force on it moves in a straight line (Newton's first law). Gravity is what bends our path into a circle along with the turning ground.
- With no gravity, you would keep going along the straight line (the tangent) while the curved ground turns away. The gap after time t is about (v·t)² / (2R). For v = 465 m/s, t = 5 s and R = 6,378 km, that is about 0.42 m, so "about forty centimetres" at the equator. This ignores the air. Everything on the ground (people, cars, the water in the oceans) does the same.

**The air**
- Air pressure at the ground (about 101 kPa) is the weight of the whole column of air above each square metre.
- That pressure pushes the air upward, and gravity normally balances it. Without gravity, nothing holds the air down. The atmosphere starts accelerating upward, at first at about 9.8 m/s², and its top escapes into space. So the air rises faster every second.

**The ground**
- Rock deep underground is squeezed by the weight of the rock above it: the pressure grows by roughly 25–30 MPa per kilometre of depth.
- Rock is elastic. If that weight vanished, the compressed rock would expand (spring back) everywhere at once and send waves through the ground, which is the shaking shown. The exact size of the shaking is not known: this is a thought experiment.

**The Space Station**
- The ISS orbits at about 400 km, where gravity is still about 90% as strong as on the ground.
- Astronauts float because they and the station are in free fall around Earth: they fall toward Earth all the time, but move sideways fast enough (about 7.7 km/s) to keep missing it.
- With no gravity, the station and the crew would simply move in a straight line together, so inside the station nothing would feel different.

**When gravity comes back**
- Objects that drifted up fall back. From about 0.4 m this is a short drop, so people land with a bump, not a dangerous fall.
- The air that started rising is pulled back down. That sudden movement of the whole atmosphere would cause strong pressure changes and winds. The exact strength is uncertain (thought experiment).
