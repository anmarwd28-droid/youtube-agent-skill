## Facts (approximate, as narrated)
- Earth's field is made by the geodynamo: liquid iron (with nickel) flowing in the outer core, about 2,900–5,100 km down.
- Compasses align with the field. Sea turtles, many migratory birds and other animals use the geomagnetic field to navigate.
- The magnetosphere deflects most of the solar wind, a stream of charged particles from the Sun. Without it, the particles would reach the upper atmosphere directly.
- Satellites would get more radiation exposure and damage. Even with the field, solar storms already harm satellite electronics.
- Mars lost its global magnetic field about 4 billion years ago. NASA's MAVEN mission measured how the solar wind strips its upper atmosphere, and scientists think this helped turn Mars dry and thin-aired. How much a field protects a planet's air is still studied (Venus has no global field but keeps a thick atmosphere), so "could strip away some" is said with care.
- The field has reversed many times. Magnetic stripes on the sea floor record these flips, and the last full reversal was about 780,000 years ago. Life survived them.
