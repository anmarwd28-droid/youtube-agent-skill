## Facts (as narrated)
- **1 September 1939 – 2 September 1945:** the war began with the German invasion of Poland and ended with Japan's surrender: six years. It was the deadliest war in history.
- **More than 70 million dead:** estimates run from about 70 to 85 million, soldiers and civilians. The world population in 1940 was about 2.3 billion, so 70–85 million is about 3–3.7%: "about three of every hundred people".
- **Families that never existed:** a consequence of the deaths; no number is claimed.
- **Warsaw:** by January 1945 about 85% of the city (most of the left-bank city and the Old Town) had been destroyed; the Old Town was rebuilt after the war (UNESCO World Heritage site since 1980).
- **Hiroshima and Nagasaki:** atomic bombs were dropped on 6 and 9 August 1945. The domed building shown is the Hiroshima Prefectural Industrial Promotion Hall (today the Atomic Bomb Dome), drawn as it was before the bombing.
- **Penicillin:** discovered by Alexander Fleming in 1928. Mass production was developed in the US and UK during the war; about 2.3 million doses were ready for the Normandy landings (June 1944).
- **The first electronic computers:** Colossus (Bletchley Park, 1944) was built to break German teleprinter codes; ENIAC (USA, completed 1945) was built to compute artillery firing tables.
- **The first nuclear reactor:** Chicago Pile-1, a pile of graphite bricks and uranium, went critical on 2 December 1942 as part of the Manhattan Project.
- **The first rocket to reach space:** a V-2 (test flight MW 18014, 20 June 1944) reached about 176 km, above the 100 km Kármán line.
- **More than 80 colonies:** the UN says that since its founding in 1945 more than 80 former colonies have gained independence. The war weakened the European empires (Britain, France, the Netherlands, Belgium, Portugal); India (1947) and Indonesia (1945/1949) came right after it. The dots show about 80 capitals with their independence years (map simplified). "Maybe for decades longer" is speculation, said as "ربّما".
- **The microwave:** Percy Spencer, a Raytheon engineer working on magnetrons for radar, noticed in 1945 that a chocolate bar in his pocket had melted near an active magnetron; Raytheon filed the patent in 1945 and sold the first microwave oven ("Radarange") in 1947. The cavity magnetron itself was invented in 1940 (Randall and Boot, Birmingham) for wartime radar.
