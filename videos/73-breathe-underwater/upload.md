## Facts (numbers and sources)
- Oceans cover ≈71% of Earth's surface; more than 80% of the ocean is unmapped/unexplored (NOAA).
- Oxygen: 1 L of air holds ≈0.28 g O₂ (21% by volume); 1 L of water at 15–20 °C holds ≈0.007–0.009 g dissolved O₂ → ≈30–40× less.
- At rest a person uses ≈250 mL O₂/min (≈0.36 g). With ≈7 mg/L and ~80% extraction (fish gills), that needs ≈60 L of water a minute; resting breathing moves only ≈6 L of air a minute.
- Water density ≈1000 kg/m³ vs air ≈1.2 kg/m³ → ≈800× heavier; viscosity ≈50× higher.
- Water conducts heat ≈24× better than air (0.6 vs 0.025 W/m·K); body heat is lost ≈25× faster in water.
- Pressure rises by ≈1 atmosphere every 10 m of seawater (the weight of the whole atmosphere ≈ 10 m of water).
- Liquid is practically incompressible: liquid-filled lungs don't squeeze (Boyle's law applies to gas); liquid breathing (perfluorocarbons) has been tested since the 1960s (Kylstra, Clark).
- Deepest open-sea dive: 534 m (Comex Hydra 8, 1988).
- Human embryos at about 4–5 weeks have pharyngeal arches, folds in the neck that are homologous to fish gill arches (they become jaw, ear and throat parts).
