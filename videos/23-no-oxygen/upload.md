## Facts (as narrated)

The scenario is that only the free oxygen gas (O₂) in the air disappears for 5 seconds. Oxygen bound inside molecules stays.

**Air and pressure**
- Dry air is about 78% nitrogen, 20.95% oxygen and about 1% argon and others. Oxygen is about one fifth of the air.
- Removing it lowers sea-level pressure from about 101 kPa to about 80 kPa.
- In the standard atmosphere, 80 kPa is the pressure at about 1,900–2,000 m. A sudden change like that makes the ears "pop".

**Fire and engines**
- Combustion needs oxygen, and flames go out when the oxygen is gone. Hot embers stay hot and can reignite when oxygen returns.
- Car engines burn fuel with oxygen from the air, so they stall.
- Jet engines can "flame out", and pilots have in-flight relight procedures to restart them.

**People**
- Blood already carries oxygen.
- Even with a sudden total loss of oxygen at extreme altitude, the "time of useful consciousness" is about 9–12 seconds (FAA, at 50,000 ft).
- Five seconds is too short to harm a healthy person.

**The surprise (correcting a viral myth)**
- Oxygen in water (H₂O) and in building materials (silicates and carbonates in concrete, stone and glass) is chemically bound. It is not free O₂ gas.
- So water would not vanish and buildings would not crumble.
