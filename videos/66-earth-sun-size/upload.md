## Facts (numbers and formulas)
- The Sun's radius is 696,000 km, about 109 Earth radii (6,371 km); its volume is about 1.3 million Earths (109³ ≈ 1.3 × 10⁶) (NASA Sun fact sheet).
- The Moon orbits at an average 384,400 km, less than the new Earth radius of 696,000 km, so the Moon would be inside the planet.
- Mean density: Earth 5.51 g/cm³, Sun 1.41 g/cm³; ratio ≈ 3.9 («أثقلُ منها بأربعِ مرّات»).
- Mass of a Sun-sized body with Earth's density: M = ρ·V = 5.51 × (4/3)π(6.96 × 10⁸ m)³ ≈ 7.8 × 10³⁰ kg ≈ 3.9 solar masses (Sun 1.99 × 10³⁰ kg) («نحوَ أربعِ شموسٍ»).
- Surface gravity g = (4/3)πGρR, so at the same density g grows with R: 109 × 9.8 ≈ 1,070 m/s². A 70 kg person would weigh like 70 × 109 ≈ 7,630 kg («أكثرَ من سبعةِ أطنان»).
- Two-body system: with 3.9 solar masses against 1, the barycentre lies close to the Earth, so the Sun moves around it.
- A star like the Sun is held up by the thermal pressure of its hot gas (hydrostatic equilibrium, heated by fusion). Cold matter can be held up only by electron degeneracy up to the Chandrasekhar limit (about 1.4 solar masses) and by neutron degeneracy up to about 2–3 solar masses (TOV limit). 3.9 solar masses of cold rock and iron is above both, so it would collapse.
- Free-fall time t = √(3π / 32Gρ) with ρ = 5,510 kg/m³ ≈ 900 s ≈ 15 minutes («خلالَ دقائقَ معدودة»; the real collapse would release huge energy, so the outcome is said with «قد»).
- Schwarzschild radius r = 2GM/c² = 2.95 km × 3.9 ≈ 11.5 km, so the black hole would be about 23 km across («نحوُ ثلاثةٍ وعشرينَ كيلومتراً»).
- A black hole of the same mass pulls on distant bodies like the same mass did before, so the Sun and the planets would still orbit it.
