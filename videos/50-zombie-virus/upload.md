## Facts (as narrated)
- **The zombie-ant fungus:** *Ophiocordyceps unilateralis* infects carpenter ants; the ant leaves its colony, climbs plants, clamps its mandibles on the underside of a leaf (often on a vein) in a "death grip" and dies there; a fungal stalk grows from the back of its head and drops spores (Hughes et al., BMC Ecology 2011; Fredericksen et al., PNAS 2017).
- **Rabies:** a lyssavirus (bullet-shaped, about 180 × 75 nm) that travels along nerves to the brain; symptoms include agitation, aggression, confusion and hydrophobia; it spreads mainly through the saliva of bites from infected animals; almost always fatal once symptoms start; about 59,000 deaths a year (WHO). Human-to-human spread by bites is extremely rare; the video only says the virus spreads by biting.
- **Doubling:** each infected person infects one new person a day, so the number doubles every day: 2^33 = 8,589,934,592, more than the world population (about 8.2 billion) after 33 days (simple model, "in theory").
- **Flights:** about 100,000 commercial flights a day worldwide (pre-pandemic figures about 100,000–120,000 a day; IATA, ICAO). People can fly during an incubation period before symptoms.
- **Cities first, remote areas last:** in a statistical-mechanics model of a zombie outbreak in the USA, dense cities fell within days, while remote, thinly populated areas held out for months (Alemi et al., Cornell, 2015, "You Can Run, You Can Hide").
- **Blackout:** power grids and data centres need people to run, repair and fuel them; with no workers, outages spread within days (illustrative, no exact number narrated).
- **The twist:** rabies patients have painful throat spasms when they try to swallow, so they fear and avoid water (hydrophobia). A person usually survives only about 3 days without water (it varies with heat and activity).
- **The vaccine:** Louis Pasteur first used a rabies vaccine on a person, Joseph Meister, on 6 July 1885; modern rabies vaccines are safe and very effective, even after a bite if given quickly.
