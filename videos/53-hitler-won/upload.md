## Facts (as narrated)
- **1942:** at their largest (November 1942) Germany and its allies held most of Europe, from France (all of it occupied after November 1942) to the Volga at Stalingrad and the Caucasus. The map is simplified (neutral Spain, Switzerland, Sweden and Turkey are left out).
- **Stalingrad:** the German 6th Army surrendered at Stalingrad on 2 February 1943, the turning point in the East. Fuel was Germany's chronic shortage (the 1942 drive to the Caucasus was for oil; by 1944 the Luftwaffe was grounded for lack of fuel).
- **About 300,000 planes:** the United States built roughly 300,000 military aircraft from 1940 to 1945 (about 324,000 by some counts); Germany built about 120,000.
- **The Holocaust:** about six million Jews were murdered, and millions of others (Roma, Soviet prisoners of war, Poles, disabled people and more). A victory would have let it go on.
- **Tens of millions driven out:** Generalplan Ost, the Nazi plan for Eastern Europe, foresaw deporting or killing tens of millions of Slavs (one version: about 31 million) to make room for German settlers.
- **United Nations / human rights:** the UN was founded in 1945 by the Allies who won the war; the Universal Declaration of Human Rights was adopted in 1948. "Would not have been born" is speculation, phrased as a consequence of the counterfactual.
- **The first rocket in space:** a V-2 (test flight MW 18014, 20 June 1944, from Peenemünde) reached about 176 km, above the 100 km Kármán line: the first human-made object in space.
- **von Braun:** Wernher von Braun led the V-2 design (used as a weapon from September 1944); after the war he went to the United States and led the design of the Saturn V that carried Apollo crews to the Moon (Apollo 11, 1969).
- **"Rockets might have stayed weapons":** speculation, said as "ربّما".
- **The bomb:** the Einstein–Szilárd letter to President Roosevelt (2 August 1939) warned that Germany might build an atomic bomb; this led to the US programme, which became the Manhattan Project in 1942.
- **Fission in Berlin:** Otto Hahn and Fritz Strassmann discovered nuclear fission in Berlin in December 1938 (Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Chemistry); Lise Meitner and Otto Frisch explained it.
- **Forced to leave:** the 1933 civil-service law and later persecution drove out many leading scientists. Einstein left Germany in 1933 and never returned; Lise Meitner fled in July 1938.
- **Built with scientists who fled:** Enrico Fermi (left Italy in 1938; his wife was Jewish), Leó Szilárd, Hans Bethe, Otto Frisch and others who had fled fascism worked on the bomb (Bethe led the theory division at Los Alamos). Meitner refused to work on it.
