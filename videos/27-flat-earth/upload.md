# ماذا لو كانت الأرض مسطّحة؟ — Short #27

64 s, 1080x1920, 30 fps. Global framing. Every scene is real 3D, built only in three.js (no outside assets).

What it shows, in order:
1. The 3D globe (the same three.js Earth as the published Shorts: day side, city lights, clouds, a blue atmosphere rim) in a navy, starry space flattens into a thick rock disc.
2. 3D gold arrows point inward from the rim, and gravity folds the disc back into a sphere.
3. On the disc, 3D gravity arrows all point to the centre, and the "straight down" arrows are crossed out.
4. A 3D city street (procedural buildings, shops, trees with leaves, lamp posts, sunlight and shadows) tilts more and more. People walk as if climbing, a ball rolls back toward the centre, and a slope gauge rises.
5. The oceans slide inward: a raised, glossy sea in the middle of the disc, with dry, pale rims.
6. Night with city lights over the whole disc. The Sun rises and the entire disc lights up at the same moment.
7. A 3D container ship on a real three.js ocean (waves, sky reflections) sails away, staying whole and only shrinking until haze hides it.
8. The edge: the camera flies over a rock plateau with boulders, past the rim, and looks down a layered cliff into black, starry space.
9. A lunar eclipse on the 3D Moon: Earth's round shadow, with its full circle outlined.
10. The flat disc with the question mark and the subscribe button.

## Facts (as narrated)

- **An Earth-sized body can't stay flat:** gravity pulls all its mass toward its centre, so any body above a few hundred km across settles into a sphere (hydrostatic equilibrium). Rocky bodies become round at about 600 km in diameter, icy ones at about 400 km (e.g. Ceres at about 940 km is round). Earth is about 12,742 km.
- **Gravity on a disc points to the centre of the disc:** away from the middle, the pull has a sideways part toward the centre. The ground would feel more and more tilted, like walking uphill (standard result for the gravity of a thin disc).
- **Water and air collect at the middle:** they flow "downhill" toward the centre of the pull, leaving a central sea and dry, airless rims.
- **No day and night as we know them:** a flat surface facing the Sun is lit everywhere at the same moment. On a sphere, half is always in night.
- **Ships and the horizon:** on a sphere, ships disappear hull-first behind the curve (the horizon is about 5 km away for a person 2 m tall). On a flat Earth they would only shrink until haze hides them.
- **The edge:** in the common flat-Earth map (an azimuthal projection centred on the North Pole), travelling away from the north leads to the rim.
- **Proof that Earth is round:** in every lunar eclipse, Earth's shadow on the Moon is curved, the edge of a circle, whatever the Moon's position. Only a sphere casts a round shadow from every angle. Aristotle used this argument around 350 BC.
