## Facts (approximate, as narrated)
- Earth's surface moves about 1,670 km/h at the equator (40,075 km per ~23 h 56 min); at Baghdad (33°N) about 1,400 km/h (×cos 33°). A jet airliner cruises at ~900 km/h.
- If the solid Earth stopped suddenly, anything not fixed to bedrock — and the atmosphere and oceans — would keep moving east at that speed: winds far beyond any hurricane and huge ocean surges.
- If it stayed stopped, the equatorial bulge (~21 km) would no longer hold the water: over time the oceans would gather into two polar oceans, with one continuous continent around the equator (as modelled by Esri / Witold Fraczek).
- Still orbiting the Sun, one day would last one year: about six months of daylight and six months of night.
- Nothing can stop Earth suddenly; tidal friction from the Moon lengthens the day by roughly 1.7–2.3 milliseconds per century.
