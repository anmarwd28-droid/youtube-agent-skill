## Facts (numbers and sources)
- The human body holds about 38 trillion bacteria and about 30 trillion human cells (Sender, Fuchs & Milo, PLoS Biology 2016: "Revised estimates for the number of human and bacteria cells in the body"), so bacteria are slightly more numerous («نحوُ ثمانيةٍ وثلاثينَ تريليونَ بكتيريا، أكثرُ من خلاياكَ نفسِها»).
- World health spending reached about US$9.8 trillion in 2021, about 10% of global GDP (WHO, Global spending on health 2023) («نحوَ عشرةِ تريليوناتِ دولارٍ»).
- Of about 55 million deaths in 2019, noncommunicable diseases caused 74% and communicable, maternal, perinatal and nutritional conditions about 18%; injuries about 8% (WHO Global Health Estimates 2019). Disease therefore causes well over 80% of deaths («أكثرُ من ثمانينَ في المئةِ»).
- Road traffic crashes kill about 1.19 million people a year (WHO Global status report on road safety 2023) («أكثرَ من مليونِ إنسانٍ»). Without disease, injuries such as road crashes would become the leading cause of death.
- The world has tens of millions of health workers (WHO: about 65 million, including about 28 million nurses, State of the World's Nursing 2020).
- Ageing is not a disease: without illness the body still ages; the longest documented human life is 122 years (Jeanne Calment, 1875–1997), so "many would pass 100" is framed as «قد».
- Animals and plants have their own diseases (for example foot-and-mouth disease in cattle, fungal leaf spots in plants), so germs would not disappear from the planet.
