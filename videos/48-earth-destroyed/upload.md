## Facts (as narrated)
Scenario: the Earth is suddenly blown apart into rocks flying outward faster than its escape speed.
- **A week of the Sun's whole output:** the gravitational binding energy of the Earth is about 2.2–2.5 × 10³² J (3GM²/5R ≈ 2.24 × 10³² J for a uniform sphere; ≈ 2.5 × 10³² J with the real density profile). The Sun gives out 3.83 × 10²⁶ W, so 2.5 × 10³² ÷ 3.83 × 10²⁶ ≈ 6.5 × 10⁵ s ≈ 7.5 days, "a whole week".
- **The rocks hit the Moon a few hours later:** to escape, the rocks must leave faster than 11.2 km/s; the Moon is 384,400 km away: 384,400 ÷ 15 km/s ≈ 7 hours (shown as «بعدَ نحوِ ٧ ساعات»).
- **The Moon does not fall:** it orbits the Earth at about 1 km/s while the Earth–Moon pair orbits the Sun at about 30 km/s; without the Earth the Moon simply keeps going around the Sun on nearly the Earth's old orbit, as a small planet.
- **The only known life:** no life has been found anywhere except on the Earth.
- **The light reaches Mars after 3 to 22 minutes:** Mars is 0.37–2.7 AU from the Earth (55–400 million km); light needs about 3 to 22 minutes. From Mars the Earth looks like a bright blue-white star.
- **Radio for more than a hundred years:** public radio broadcasting began around 1920; those signals now fill a bubble about 100 light-years wide in radius. Star distances shown: Proxima Centauri 4.2, Sirius 8.6, Vega 25, Arcturus 37, Aldebaran 65 light-years.
- **Voyager 1 and 2:** launched in 1977, each carries a gold-plated record with sounds and images of the Earth and greetings in 55 languages. Voyager 1 is more than 25 billion km away and still travelling.
- **A ring around the Sun that may strike Mars and Venus for millions of years:** rocks leaving at just above escape speed stay on orbits close to the Earth's, spread along it into a ring, and cross the paths of Venus and Mars; such rocks are swept up by collisions over millions of years (narrated as "may").
- **The twist:** in about 5 billion years the Sun leaves the main sequence and swells into a red giant; at its largest (about 7.6 billion years from now) it may swallow the Earth (Schröder & Smith 2008). Narrated as "may".
