## Facts (numbers and sources)
- A 70 kg adult carries about 3.8 × 10^13 bacteria and about 3.0 × 10^13 human cells (Sender, Fuchs & Milo, PLOS Biology 2016).
- Gut bacteria help break down fibre the body cannot digest, make some vitamins (e.g. vitamin K) and crowd out pathogens.
- Tuberculosis killed about 1.25 million people in 2023, the top infectious killer (WHO Global TB Report 2024).
- Bacterial infections were associated with about 7.7 million deaths in 2019, about 13.6 % or one in eight of all deaths (GBD 2019 Antimicrobial Resistance Collaborators, The Lancet 2022).
- Cholera (Vibrio cholerae), plague (Yersinia pestis) and much food poisoning (Salmonella, Campylobacter, some E. coli) are bacterial.
- Antibiotic-resistant bacteria directly caused about 1.14 million deaths in 2021 (GRAM, The Lancet 2024); infections are a main risk of surgery.
- Staphylococcus aureus lives harmlessly in the noses of about 30 % of people but can cause deadly blood infections (sepsis).
- Most E. coli strains are harmless gut residents; some (e.g. O157:H7) cause severe illness.
- The immune system learns from early contact with microbes; the "hygiene"/"old friends" hypothesis links too little microbial exposure to more allergies (e.g. to pollen) — a hypothesis, so the narration says «قد».
