## Facts (approximate, as narrated)
- About 80% of the world's cropland is rain-fed and it produces roughly 60% of the world's food (FAO).
- Reservoirs and rivers buffer the first weeks; topsoil moisture falls within weeks without rain, and rain-fed crops wilt.
- The Tigris and Euphrates are fed mostly by rain and snowmelt in their upper basins (Turkey, Iran, Syria); without precipitation their flow would fall to a fraction, leaving exposed, cracked riverbeds (as in recent Iraqi droughts).
- Hydropower output falls with reservoir level and flow (e.g. Mosul and Haditha dams).
- Without recharge, heavy pumping lowers groundwater tables, so wells must be drilled deeper and shallow ones run dry.
- Dry vegetation burns more easily; dry, bare soil feeds dust storms.
- Very dry, crusted or compacted soil absorbs water slowly, so heavy rain after a drought runs off and causes flash floods.
