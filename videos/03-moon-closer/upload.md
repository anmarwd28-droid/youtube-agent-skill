## Facts used (approximate, as said in the narration)
- Average Earth–Moon distance is about 384,400 km.
- At half the distance the Moon looks twice as wide and about four times as bright (area).
- Tidal force scales with 1/distance³, so at half the distance tides are about 8× stronger; tides come twice a day.
- Strong tidal stress on the solid Earth would raise earthquakes and volcanism (as on Jupiter's moon Io).
- The Moon is really moving away by about 3.8 cm per year (lunar laser ranging).
