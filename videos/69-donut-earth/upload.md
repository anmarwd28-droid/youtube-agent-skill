## Facts (numbers and sources)
- A torus planet with Earth's mass can only hold its shape if it spins fast: a day of about 2.8 hours (Anders Sandberg, "Torus-Earth", 2014 analysis), so 8+ sunrises in one of our days.
- Surface gravity varies: strongest near the "poles" (top and bottom of the ring), roughly 2/3 g; weakest on the outer equator, under 1/3 g once the spin is counted (Sandberg). Jump height ∝ 1/g, so more than 3× higher.
- From inside the hole the far side of the ring is overhead as an arch.
- Fast spin → stronger Coriolis forces → more violent storms.
- No known natural process makes torus planets: above ~400 km bodies pull themselves into spheres (hydrostatic equilibrium); a torus is unstable and could break up after a large impact.
