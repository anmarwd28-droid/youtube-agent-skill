## Facts (as narrated)

- **Antimatter is a mirror copy of matter:** same mass, opposite charge. The positron (anti-electron, positive) was predicted by Dirac (1928) and found by Carl Anderson (1932); the antiproton (negative) was found at Berkeley in 1955.
- **Annihilation turns all the mass into energy:** E = mc², with both the matter and the antimatter mass converted (electron + positron → two gamma photons of 511 keV each, flying in opposite directions). Fission turns about 0.1% of the mass into energy and fusion about 0.7%, hence «أقلّ من ١٪».
- **The air would hit the skin and annihilate,** giving off gamma rays (and, from nuclei, pions that decay to gamma rays), which are deadly in large doses.
- **One gram ≈ three Hiroshima bombs:** 1 g antimatter + 1 g matter: E = 0.002 kg × (3×10⁸ m/s)² = 1.8×10¹⁴ J ≈ 43 kilotons of TNT (1 kt = 4.184×10¹² J). Hiroshima ≈ 15 kt, so about 2.9 times.
- **The whole body ≈ 3,000 megatons, 60 times the strongest bomb:** 70 kg antimatter + 70 kg matter: E = 140 kg × 9×10¹⁶ = 1.26×10¹⁹ J ≈ 3,000 Mt. The Tsar Bomba (1961), the strongest bomb ever detonated, was about 50 Mt: 3,000 / 50 = 60. (Said as "if all of it annihilated"; in reality the first blast would scatter much of the body.)
- **The Big Bang made nearly equal amounts;** about one extra particle of matter per billion survived the annihilation (the baryon asymmetry, about 1 in 10⁹, measured from the ratio of baryons to photons in the cosmic microwave background). Everything we see is made of that leftover.
- **CERN makes antimatter** (antihydrogen since 1995, trapped by ALPHA in 2010 in magnetic traps). CERN: "If we could assemble all of the antimatter we've ever made at CERN and annihilate it with matter, we would have enough energy to light a single electric light bulb for a few minutes." The LHC ring is 27 km around.
- **Your body makes antimatter:** potassium-40 (about 0.012% of all potassium) decays by positron emission in about 0.001% of decays. A body with about 140–160 g of potassium has about 4,000–5,000 K-40 decays a second, so roughly 150–180 positrons an hour, hence «أكثرَ من مئة». Each meets an electron at once and annihilates. Bananas are rich in potassium (a banana gives off a positron about every 75 minutes; Symmetry magazine, Fermilab/SLAC).
- **Global framing:** the street and the people stand for anyone in the world.
