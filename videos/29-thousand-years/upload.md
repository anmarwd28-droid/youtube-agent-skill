## Facts (as narrated)

- **Record:** Jeanne Calment lived 122 years and 164 days (1875–1997), the longest verified human life. 1000 / 122 ≈ 8.2, narrated as "eight times".
- **Heartbeats:** 70 beats/min × 60 × 24 × 365.25 × 1000 ≈ 36.8 billion, narrated as "about 37 billion". A life of about 80 years is about 3 billion.
- **Teeth:** humans grow two sets only (20 milk teeth, then 32 permanent). Enamel has no living cells once formed, so it cannot regrow.
- **Generations:** with about 30 years per generation, 1000 / 30 ≈ 33 generations would overlap; narrated as "up to the thirtieth generation".
- **Population:** about 132 million births a year. With no deaths, 8.2 billion more people would take about 8.2 / 0.132 ≈ 62 years (at a constant number of births); narrated as "sixty years".
- **Accidents:** with no ageing and a constant yearly risk of death of 1 in 1000, the share alive after 1000 years is (1 − 0.001)^1000 ≈ e⁻¹ ≈ 37%, narrated as "about a third".
- **Greenland shark:** radiocarbon dating of eye lenses gave an age of about 392 ± 120 years for the largest animal (Nielsen et al., Science, 2016): the longest-lived vertebrate known.
