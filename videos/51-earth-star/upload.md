## Facts (as narrated)
- **Why the Sun shines:** fusion of hydrogen into helium (the proton–proton chain: four hydrogen nuclei make one helium nucleus plus energy) in the core at about 15 million °C (15.7 million K; NASA Sun fact sheet).
- **The Earth's core:** about 5,200–6,000 °C at the centre and the inner-core boundary (Anzellini et al., Science 2013: about 6,000 °C). 15,000,000 ÷ 6,000 = 2,500.
- **The mass needed to fuse hydrogen:** about 0.075–0.08 solar masses (about 75–80 Jupiter masses). The Sun is 333,000 Earth masses, so 0.075 × 333,000 ≈ 25,000 and 0.08 × 333,000 ≈ 26,600 Earth masses: "more than 25,000 times". A ball of 25,000 Earths at the same density would be 25,000^(1/3) ≈ 29 times wider (the on-screen ball).
- **What they are made of:** the Sun (photosphere, mass fractions): hydrogen ≈ 74%, helium ≈ 25%, the rest ≈ 1% (Asplund et al. 2009). The bulk Earth: iron 32.1%, oxygen 30.1%, silicon 15.1%, magnesium 13.9%, the rest ≈ 9% (Morgan & Anders 1980; McDonough & Sun 1995); hydrogen is only a tiny trace.
- **The Moon falling:** if the Earth's mass jumped to 26,600 times its value, the pull at the Moon's distance would be 26,600 times stronger ("thousands of times"). The Moon's speed (about 1 km/s) is tiny next to the new circular speed (about 166 km/s), so it falls almost straight in. Free-fall time t = (π/2)·√(r³ / (2GM)) with r = 384,400 km and M = 26,600 Earth masses ≈ 2,570 s ≈ 43 minutes: "less than an hour".
- **Oceans boil, life ends:** falling gas and the hot new surface heat the planet to thousands of degrees (a red dwarf's surface is about 2,300–2,700 °C), far above the boiling point of water and the melting point of rock (about 1,000–1,200 °C).
- **Ignition and size:** a newborn star near the minimum mass takes hundreds of millions of years to contract and settle into steady hydrogen burning ("millions of years"); the smallest stars are about the size of Jupiter (about 0.1 solar radius ≈ 1 Jupiter radius; e.g. TRAPPIST-1 ≈ 1.2 Jupiter radii).
- **From Mars:** a red dwarf of about 0.0001–0.0005 solar luminosity seen from Mars (0.5–2.5 AU away) shines at roughly magnitude −14 to −19 (m = −26.74 − 2.5·log L + 5·log d), far brighter than the full Moon from Earth (−12.7): a second, small red sun in the sky. Mars sunsets have a bluish glow around the Sun (NASA rovers).
- **The twist:** the smallest red dwarfs burn slowly and are fully convective; models give 6 to 12 trillion years on the main sequence (Laughlin, Bodenheimer & Adams 1997), while the Sun's main-sequence life is about 10 billion years: more than 600 times longer.
