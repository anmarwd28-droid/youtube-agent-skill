## Facts (numbers and sources)
- Walking needs static friction: the foot pushes the ground backward and friction pushes the body forward (Newton's third law). Without it the feet slide and a walking person keeps sliding with the speed they had (Newton's first law).
- Tyres steer and brakes stop a car only through friction (brake pad on disc, tyre on road). Without it a moving car keeps going in a straight line at the same speed.
- Knots hold, and nails stay in wood, because of friction; without it a loaded knot slips open and a loaded nail slides out.
- Air drag is fluid friction. Raindrops fall at their terminal speed, at most ≈ 9 m/s for the largest drops (Gunn & Kinzer 1949). Without drag they accelerate at g: in 5 s they gain 9.8 × 5 ≈ 49 m/s, reaching ≈ 51–58 m/s ≈ 185–210 km/h, so "about 200 km/h".
- Soil and rocks stay on slopes because of friction; without it they slide (the angle of repose is set by the friction coefficient).
- When friction returns, sliding objects decelerate and their kinetic energy turns into heat.
- Articular cartilage in synovial joints has a friction coefficient of about 0.001–0.03 (e.g. Mow & Ateshian; Forster & Fisher 1996), lower than ice sliding on ice (about 0.02–0.1 depending on temperature and speed).
