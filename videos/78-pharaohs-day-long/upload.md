## Facts (as narrated), with sources
- **4,500 years ago / Khufu:** the Great Pyramid was built for Khufu around 2560 BC (≈4,580 years before 2026).
- **146 m, like a 45-storey building:** original height ≈146.6 m (now ≈138.5 m); at about 3.2 m per storey that is ≈45 storeys.
- **≈2.3 million blocks, average over two tonnes:** standard estimates of 2.3 million blocks averaging ≈2.5 t (Lehner, *The Complete Pyramids*). A car is ≈1.5 t.
- **White polished casing:** the faces were cased in fine white Tura limestone, mostly removed in the Middle Ages (Cairo, after the 1303 earthquake); some casing stones remain at the base.
- **Paid workers, not slaves:** the workers' town at Heit el-Ghurab with bakeries and huge quantities of cattle, sheep and goat bones (Lehner, AERA); the workers' cemetery beside the plateau with tombs near the king's pyramid (Hawass). Crew names painted on blocks include "Friends of Khufu"; "Drunkards of Menkaure" is from Menkaure's pyramid, built later.
- **Wet sand halves the pulling force:** Fall et al., *Physical Review Letters* 112, 175502 (2014), University of Amsterdam: the right amount of water roughly halves the force needed to pull a sledge on sand; the tomb of Djehutihotep (Middle Kingdom, ≈1900 BC) shows a man pouring water in front of a colossus on a sledge.
- **Granite from Aswan, more than 800 km by river, some blocks tens of tonnes:** the King's Chamber is lined with Aswan granite; its roof beams weigh ≈25–80 t. Aswan to Giza is ≈680 km in a straight line and ≈900 km along the Nile.
- **Copper chisels and hard-stone hammers:** copper tools and dolerite pounders were the main tools of the Old Kingdom.
- **True north within a tenth of a degree:** the Great Pyramid's sides deviate from the cardinal directions by about 3–4 arc minutes (≈0.06°) (Spence, *Nature* 2000).
- **Merer's diary:** the Wadi al-Jarf papyri (year 27 of Khufu), the oldest inscribed papyri found so far, record Merer's team carrying limestone from Tura across the Nile and canals to Giza (Tallet, 2013).
- **Sirius and the flood; the 365-day calendar:** the heliacal rising of Sirius before dawn announced the inundation; the civil calendar had 12 months of 30 days plus 5 extra (epagomenal) feast days.
- **The hippo:** adults weigh about 1.5–4 t and are known to attack boats; Nile crocodiles lived in the river.
- **Beer and bread:** beer was a daily, thick, low-alcohol drink; grit from grinding stones and sand in the flour wore down the teeth of mummies, even in young adults.
- **Sleeping on roofs; mud-brick houses with small high windows; wooden headrests:** standard features of Egyptian houses and furniture (e.g., Deir el-Medina, Amarna).
- **The Edwin Smith papyrus:** a surgical text with 48 injury cases, each with examination and treatment (copy ≈1600 BC of an older text). Splints of wood bound with linen are known from Egyptian burials; honey on wounds is in the medical papyri, and modern studies confirm honey's antibacterial effect. Healed fractures are found in the workers' skeletons at Giza. The Ebers papyrus includes remedies with animal dung.
- **No money:** trade was barter; the first coins were struck in Lydia ≈600 BC, about 2,000 years after the pyramid.
- **Kohl:** worn by men and women; Tapsoba et al., *Analytical Chemistry* 2010 found that lead salts in kohl make skin cells produce nitric oxide, which may help fight eye infections (narrated as "may help").
- **Wigs and shaved heads:** many wealthy Egyptians shaved their heads and wore wigs, against lice and heat.
- **Scribes and hieroglyphs:** only a small minority could read and write; hieroglyphic writing used hundreds of signs, some for single sounds (like the owl for m) and some for whole words (the sun disc for "sun").
- **Senet:** a board of 30 squares, one of the oldest board games known (from ≈3100 BC).
- **Life expectancy:** about 30 years on average, mainly because of child mortality; people who survived childhood often reached 40–50 or more.
- **Tallest building for ≈3,800 years:** until Lincoln Cathedral's spire (≈160 m, completed 1311).
- **Cleopatra:** Cleopatra VII (69–30 BC) lived ≈2,500 years after the pyramid and ≈2,000 years before the Moon landing (1969).
