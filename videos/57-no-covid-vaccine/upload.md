## Facts (as narrated)
- **Under a year, the fastest vaccine:** the SARS-CoV-2 genome was published on 10–11 January 2020; the first vaccine shot outside trials was given on 8 December 2020 (UK, Pfizer-BioNTech), about 11 months later. The previous record is usually given as the mumps vaccine, about 4 years (1963–1967). Sources: WHO; NEJM (Polack et al., 2020); History of Vaccines.
- **About 4 billion people in lockdown:** in early April 2020 more than 3.9 billion people, about half of humanity, were asked or ordered to stay at home (AFP count, April 2020).
- **Hospitals, oxygen, corridors:** in the waves of 2020–2021 hospitals in many countries were overwhelmed; several (for example in India in April–May 2021) ran out of medical oxygen and patients were treated in corridors. Sources: WHO, Lancet commentary 2021.
- **Mutations:** every replication can bring copy errors; more infections mean more chances for variants (Alpha, Delta, Omicron). Omicron (B.1.1.529) carried more than 30 mutations in the spike protein (about 32). Source: WHO technical brief, Nov 2021.
- **About 20 million lives:** Watson et al., *The Lancet Infectious Diseases* (2022): COVID-19 vaccines prevented about 19.8 million deaths (estimate from excess deaths; 14.4 million from reported deaths) in the first year of vaccination, 8 Dec 2020 – 8 Dec 2021. Most COVID-19 deaths, and so most lives saved, were among older people.
- **1.6 billion students, flights:** UNESCO: at the peak in April 2020 schools were closed for about 1.6 billion learners (over 90% of the world's students). ICAO: passenger numbers in 2020 fell by about 60% (1.8 billion vs 4.5 billion in 2019), and in April 2020 most scheduled flights were cancelled. "Without a vaccine this lasts years" is said as a consequence (the restrictions eased only as vaccination spread).
- **Immunity from infection:** infection gives immunity, but it wanes over months and reinfection is common, especially with new variants; reaching it by infection alone costs millions of deaths. Sources: WHO; Lancet 2023 meta-analysis (COVID-19 Forecasting Team).
- **Dexamethasone:** RECOVERY trial (NEJM 2021): the cheap steroid cut deaths by about one third among patients on mechanical ventilation (about one fifth among those on oxygen); it is a treatment for severe disease, it does not prevent infection.
- **Karikó:** Katalin Karikó worked on mRNA for decades; her grant applications were turned down again and again and in 1995 she was demoted at the University of Pennsylvania. With Drew Weissman she found (2005) how to modify mRNA so it does not set off inflammation, the basis of the mRNA vaccines; both received the 2023 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. Source: Nobel Prize press release, 2 Oct 2023.
- **Cancer:** personalised mRNA cancer vaccines are in clinical trials, for example mRNA-4157 (V940) with pembrolizumab in melanoma (phase 2b: lower risk of recurrence or death; phase 3 under way). Said as "being tested", not as a cure.
