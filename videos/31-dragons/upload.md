## Facts (as narrated)

- **Size limit:** the largest known flying animal, the pterosaur *Quetzalcoatlus northropi*, had a wingspan of about 10–11 m and weighed roughly 200–250 kg. Weight grows faster than wing area (square–cube law), so a flying animal cannot be the size of a house.
- **Four limbs:** all land vertebrates (tetrapods) have four limbs. In birds, bats and pterosaurs the wings are modified forelimbs, so a realistic dragon has two legs and two wings.
- **Fire:** cattle belch methane every day (roughly 200–500 litres per cow per day), a flammable gas. An animal that stored it would need an ignition source and a mouth lining that resists heat. The bombardier beetle already sprays a chemical mix at about 100 °C.
- **Flight:** birds and pterosaurs have hollow (pneumatised) bones, light for their strength, and very large chest muscles that power the wings (15–25% of a bird's body mass).
- **Food:** Kleiber's law: basal metabolism ≈ 70 × M^0.75 kcal/day. For 1,000 kg that is about 12,400 kcal; an active flyer needs several times more, about 40,000–60,000 kcal a day, or 20–30 kg of meat: about one sheep.
- **Closed airports and fire-proof roofs:** a "what if" consequence, not a measured fact.
- **Komodo dragon:** the largest living lizard, up to about 3 m long and 70–90 kg, with venom glands in its lower jaw (Fry et al., PNAS, 2009).
