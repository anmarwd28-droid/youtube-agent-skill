## Facts (as narrated)

All of this comes from Mikolajewicz, Ziemen et al. (Max Planck Institute for Meteorology), "The climate of a retrograde rotating Earth", Earth System Dynamics (2018). It was also presented at EGU 2018 and covered by Eos.

**Assumptions in the model**
- It is a climate-model experiment, not a prediction.
- Continents and ocean basins stay the same. Only the direction of rotation, and so the Coriolis effect, is reversed.
- The model ran for about 7,000 simulated years.

**Sun and winds**
- With the rotation reversed, the Sun rises in the west and sets in the east.
- Large-scale winds reverse direction.
- Northern-hemisphere cyclones and hurricanes spin clockwise, the reverse of today.

**Land: rain and deserts**
- The Sahara's desert largely disappears and turns green.
- The Middle East gets more rain.
- Large parts of the Americas become desert.
- In total, the desert area shrinks by about 11 million km² (4.2 million square miles), which is more than the area of the United States (about 9.8 million km²).

**Oceans and Europe**
- The Atlantic overturning circulation (the warm Atlantic current system) collapses, and a similar, slightly stronger circulation forms in the North Pacific.
- Easterly winds bring cold air from Russia, so western Europe gets severe winters.
- In the northern Indian Ocean, cyanobacteria (blue-green bacteria, which do not need nitrate) become the dominant producers over a large area.
