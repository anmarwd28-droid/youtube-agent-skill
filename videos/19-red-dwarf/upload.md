# ماذا لو تحوّلت الشمس فجأة إلى قزم أحمر؟ — Short #18

71 s, 1080x1920, 30 fps. Global framing.

What it shows, in order:
1. A city street at midday. The Sun shrinks to an orange-red dot and the light drains away.
2. The real 3D Sun beside a red dwarf at true relative size, with their surface temperatures. Then about 3 in every 4 stars of the galaxy light up as red dwarfs.
3. The same street at "noon": rust-brown sky, street lamps and shop lights on, people looking up, a daylight meter falling.
4. Gravity: the red dwarf (3D) with Earth on its orbit. The star's mass gauge falls to 12% and the pull arrow shrinks while the speed arrow stays.
5. Every planet flies off its orbit (top-down, not to scale).
6. Earth drifting away (3D) with the star shrinking to a dot behind it, and year and distance counters.
7. The street buried in ice under a black sky, with a thermometer below −200 °C.
8. The air freezing into snow over a dead, frosted mountain landscape under the Milky Way.
9. Rogue planets drifting through the galaxy.
10. Lifetimes compared (Sun versus red dwarf), then Proxima b: an ocean facing a huge red star and ice on the night side.
11. Our real Sun and Earth with the question mark and the subscribe button.

## Facts (approximate, as narrated)

The scenario uses a red dwarf like Proxima Centauri, the nearest star.

**Red dwarfs**
- Red dwarfs (M dwarfs) have about 0.08–0.6 solar masses and surfaces at about 2,300–3,800 K.
- They are the most common stars, roughly 70–75% of the stars in the Milky Way.
- Proxima Centauri compared with the Sun:

| | Proxima Centauri | Sun |
|---|---|---|
| Mass | about 0.12 | 1 |
| Radius | about 0.15 | 1 |
| Surface temperature | about 3,000 K (about 2,800 °C) | about 5,770 K (about 5,500 °C) |

**Sky and light**
- From Earth's distance the red dwarf would look about 6.5 times smaller across than the Sun.
- Proxima's total light output is about 0.17% of the Sun's.
- Its visible light is only about 1/18,000 of the Sun's, so daylight would drop to a few lux, darker than dusk.

**Gravity**
- If the central mass suddenly fell to about 1/8, Earth's orbital speed (29.8 km/s) would exceed the new escape speed at 1 AU (about 14.7 km/s).
- Any planet on a near-circular orbit becomes unbound once the central mass drops below half. So all the planets would leave.
- Earth would recede at about 26 km/s, roughly 820 million km a year.

**Cold**
- A drifting Earth warmed only by its interior settles near 30–50 K. That is below −200 °C, so the oceans freeze over.
- Nitrogen freezes at about −210 °C and oxygen at about −218 °C. Over thousands of years the air would condense and fall as snow and ice.

**Rogue planets**
- Microlensing surveys suggest the Milky Way holds billions of free-floating planets with no star.

**Lifetimes and Proxima b**
- Low-mass red dwarfs can shine for trillions of years. The Sun's total lifetime is about 10 billion years.
- Proxima b:
  - orbits in its star's habitable zone (about 0.05 AU, an 11-day year), 4.24 light-years away
  - is probably tidally locked
  - its habitability is uncertain because of strong stellar flares

**Our real Sun**
- It cannot become a red dwarf. In about 5 billion years it will swell into a red giant, then end as a white dwarf.
