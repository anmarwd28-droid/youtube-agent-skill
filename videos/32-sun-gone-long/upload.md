## Facts (as narrated), with sources

- **8 minutes 20 seconds:** the Sun is about 149.6 million km away; 149.6e6 / 299,792 km/s ≈ 499 s = 8 min 19 s. (NASA Sun fact sheet.)
- **Gravity travels at the speed of light:** general relativity; confirmed when gravitational waves and light from the neutron-star merger GW170817 arrived within about 2 seconds of each other after 130 million years (LIGO/Virgo, Abbott et al. 2017, ApJL 848 L13). So the Earth keeps orbiting the empty spot for the same 8 min 20 s.
- **A straight line at 107,000 km/h:** the Earth's orbital speed is 29.78 km/s × 3,600 ≈ 107,200 km/h. With no force on it, it continues along the tangent (Newton's first law). Nothing is felt: everything on Earth shares the same motion. The Moon stays bound to the Earth.
- **The Moon goes dark in moments:** it only reflects sunlight; the Earth–Moon light time is about 1.3 s.
- **Planets stay lit a little longer:** light times from the Sun: Mercury 3.2 min, Venus 6 min, Mars 12.7 min, Jupiter 43 min, Saturn 79 min. Jupiter's reflected light then needs 35–52 min to reach us, so we keep seeing it for roughly an hour after our own sky goes dark (narrated "نحو ساعة").
- **Tides:** the Sun's tide-raising force is about 46% of the Moon's, so it makes roughly a third of the combined tide (1 / (1 + 0.46) ≈ 0.68 Moon, 0.32 Sun).
- **Photosynthesis stops at once;** the temperature falls slowly at first because the air and, above all, the oceans store heat.
- **Weather:** winds and the water cycle are driven by solar heating and evaporation.
- **After a week, about 18° below zero; after a year, about 73° below zero:** David Stevenson (Caltech), quoted in Popular Science, "If the Sun went out, how long would life on Earth survive?" (2008): below 0°F (−18°C) within a week, about −100°F (−73°C) within a year. Today's global mean is about 15°C.
- **Deserts cool fastest, coasts slowest:** dry air has a weak greenhouse effect; the sea has a huge heat capacity.
- **Antarctic comparison:** the lowest air temperature measured on Earth is −89.2°C (Vostok, 21 July 1983, WMO); Antarctic interior winters average about −60 to −70°C.
- **Large trees may last years:** they store sugars and have slow metabolisms (same Popular Science piece; frost would still kill many, so narrated "قد يصمد").
- **Plankton and oxygen:** marine phytoplankton produce about half of the oxygen made each year (NOAA). The air holds about 1.2 × 10¹⁸ kg of O₂; all respiration and decay use about 3 × 10¹⁴ kg a year, so the stock would last thousands of years.
- **Grain stocks:** world cereal stocks are about 870 million tonnes against about 2,850 million tonnes used per year, a stocks-to-use ratio near 30%, so about three to four months (FAO Cereal Supply and Demand Brief).
- **Ice insulates the oceans:** sea ice forms on top and conducts heat poorly; Stevenson: the oceans would take hundreds of thousands of years to freeze solid.
- **Iceland:** about 90% of homes are heated with geothermal water (Orkustofnun, Iceland's National Energy Authority).
- **After 100 years:** shown as below the −89.2°C record and still falling (no exact figure is claimed).
- **The air freezes, after centuries (user's correction):** oxygen condenses at −183°C and nitrogen at −196°C at one atmosphere. Cooling the atmosphere and oceans to that point takes centuries to thousands of years, so it is narrated "بعد قرون".
- **A layer about 10 metres thick:** the atmosphere weighs about 10,300 kg per m²; frozen or liquid air is about 1,000 kg/m³, so 10,300 / 1,000 ≈ 10 m (narrated as "if all of it fell").
- **No air:** no sound, wind or clouds; stars do not twinkle (twinkling is caused by air); cosmic rays reach the ground.
- **Ice hundreds of metres thick after 1,000 years:** conductive growth h ≈ √(2kΔT·t / ρL) = √(2 × 3 × 230 × 3.15e10 / 3.06e8) ≈ 380 m.
- **Hydrothermal vents:** fluids up to about 400°C; discovered in 1977 at the Galápagos Rift with the submersible Alvin (Corliss et al., Science 1979); the food chain rests on chemosynthetic bacteria; giant tube worms (Riftia) live on them.
- **After a million years, about 240° below zero:** equilibrium with the Earth's internal heat flow (about 0.087 W/m²): T = (0.087 / 5.67e-8)^¼ ≈ 35 K ≈ −238°C (Stevenson gives −400°F ≈ −240°C). The oceans are frozen almost to the bottom (narrated "تقريباً").
- **About 100 light-years away:** 29.78 km/s × 3.156e13 s ≈ 9.4e14 km; one light-year is 9.46e12 km, so about 99 light-years from where the Sun was.
- **Rogue planets:** microlensing surveys suggest billions to trillions of free-floating planets in the Milky Way (Sumi et al. 2023, AJ 166, 108; NASA Roman mission summary).
- **Life goes on:** microbes live kilometres deep in rock and at vents, some on hydrogen from rock–water reactions and radiolysis, with no need for sunlight (Lin et al., Science 2006; Deep Carbon Observatory, 2018). The Earth's interior gives off about 44–47 TW, about half from radioactive decay with half-lives of billions of years (Davies & Davies 2010; KamLAND 2011).
- **The good news:** the Sun has about 5 billion years of hydrogen burning left (NASA).
