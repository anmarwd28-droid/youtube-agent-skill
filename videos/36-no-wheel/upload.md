## Facts (as narrated)

- **The first wheels, about 5,500 years ago.** Wheeled vehicles appear around 3500 BC in Mesopotamia and Europe (e.g. the Bronocice pot, ~3500 BC); the oldest surviving wooden wheel, from the Ljubljana Marshes, is about 5,150 years old. The earliest were solid discs of planks, as shown (D. W. Anthony, "The Horse, the Wheel, and Language", 2007).
- **More than a billion vehicles.** About 1.5 billion motor vehicles are in use worldwide (OICA, vehicles in use, 2020).
- **A jet engine's core is bladed wheels.** The fan, compressor and turbine of a turbofan are rotating discs with blades; without rotating machinery there is no jet engine (and no landing-gear wheels).
- **About 90% of electricity from turning generators.** Coal, gas, nuclear, hydro, wind and biomass plants all turn a generator, most through a turbine. Solar panels are the main exception, at about 7% of world electricity in 2024 (Ember, Global Electricity Review 2025). So roughly 90%+ comes from spinning machines; narrated as "نحو تسعين بالمئة".
- **Clocks with gears.** Mechanical clocks run on toothed wheels (gears); a world without the wheel has none.
- **Animals and wooden sledges.** Before the wheel, heavy loads were carried by pack animals or dragged on sledges; ancient Egyptians moved stone blocks on wooden sledges over wetted sand (Fall et al., Physical Review Letters 112, 175502, 2014).
- **Wheeled toys in the ancient Americas.** Clay animal figurines on wheels and axles were made in ancient Mesoamerica (Gulf Coast of Mexico, about 1,500–2,000 years ago), but the wheel was not used there for carts or transport.
- **Tens of thousands of kilometres of mountain roads.** The Inca road system (Qhapaq Ñan) ran for about 30,000 km through the Andes and was used by people on foot (runners called chasqui) and llama caravans, without wheeled vehicles (UNESCO World Heritage, 2014).
- **Global framing:** the street stands for any city; the ancient scenes are examples, not one country's story.
