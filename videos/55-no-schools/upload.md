## Facts (as narrated)
- **1.5 billion students:** at the peak of the COVID-19 closures (April 2020) UNESCO counted about 1.6 billion learners (over 90% of all enrolled students) affected by school closures in more than 190 countries.
- **Before schools:** for most of human history children learned by watching and copying adults and from oral stories; there were no schools before writing.
- **The first schools:** the Sumerian "tablet house" (é-dub-ba-a) where boys learned cuneiform writing; school texts survive from about 2500 BC (Shuruppak) and many from the Old Babylonian period (about 2000–1600 BC), i.e. more than 4,000 years ago.
- **Who could read:** about 12% of the world's people could read and write in 1820; today about 87% of adults can (UNESCO; Our World in Data, "Literacy").
- **Doctors:** about 12 years of school plus about 6 years of medical school (longer in some countries) before treating patients.
- **Mothers' schooling:** Gakidou et al., The Lancet 2010 (376:959–74): of the 8.2 million fewer deaths of children under 5 a year between 1970 and 2009, about 4.2 million (51%) could be attributed to more education of women of reproductive age.
- **Knowledge with its owner:** without schools, skills pass only within families or from master to apprentice; said as a possibility ("قد").
- **The word:** English school, French école, German Schule and Italian scuola come through Latin schola from the Greek σχολή (scholē), which first meant "leisure, free time", then the place where that time was spent in talk and learning.
- **A right:** Article 26 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948): "Everyone has the right to education".
