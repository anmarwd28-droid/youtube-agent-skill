## Facts (numbers and sources)
- World population about 8.2 billion in 2024–2025 (UN World Population Prospects 2024: 8.16 billion mid-2024). $1,000,000 × 8.2 billion ≈ $8.2 quadrillion = about 8,200 trillion dollars.
- World GDP 2024 about $110 trillion (IMF World Economic Outlook, Oct 2024: ≈ $110 trillion; World Bank 2024: ≈ $111 trillion). 8,200 ÷ 110 ≈ 74.5 → «أكثرُ من سبعينَ ضعفاً» (with 8.0 billion people and $111 trillion it is still ≈ 72×).
- The mechanism: more money chasing the same amount of goods raises prices (quantity theory of money, MV = PY: if the money stock jumps while output Y stays the same or falls, the price level P rises). If many people stop working, output Y falls and prices rise even more. Wealth in the economic sense is real goods and services (food, housing, work), not paper money.
- Germany (Weimar Republic) hyperinflation 1921–1923: a loaf of bread in Berlin cost around 160 marks at the end of 1922 and about 200,000,000,000 marks by late (November) 1923 (Fergusson, "When Money Dies", 1975; summarised in Encyclopaedia Britannica and Wikipedia "Hyperinflation in the Weimar Republic"). Narration: «قبلَ نحوِ مئةِ عام» (1923 = 103 years ago) and «بنحوِ مئتَي مليارِ مارك». Wheelbarrows of banknotes are a well-documented image of that time.
- Zimbabwe hyperinflation: peak in November 2008, month-on-month inflation about 79.6 billion percent, annual about 89.7 sextillion percent; prices doubled about every 24.7 hours (Hanke & Kwok, Cato Journal 2009; Hanke & Krus, "World Hyperinflations", 2012). Narration «تتضاعفُ كلَّ يومٍ تقريباً». The Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe issued a 100 trillion (100,000,000,000,000) dollar note in January 2009; the country then stopped using its own currency in 2009. The note on screen is a generic drawing (no portrait or emblem), with the value only.
- "Something like this really happened": in both cases prices exploded because far more money was printed than the goods available (Germany: printing money to pay debts and reparations; Zimbabwe: money printing during a collapse of farm output). Said as «شيءٌ يشبهُ هذا» because no country ever gave everyone a million.
- No politics, no real living people, no flags. The street, shops and people stand for any city in the world.
