60 s, 1080x1920, 30 fps. Global framing.

What it shows, in order:
1. A city street at midday. A tiny black hole appears over the road and bends the view around it.
2. A balance: the grape-size black hole on one pan and the whole 3D Earth on the other.
3. The street again, with a 1 km marker and a gravity gauge reaching 40 million times Earth's gravity.
4. Air, buildings and rocks pour towards it, heat up and glow in an accretion disk.
5. The night city lensed around the black hole (a "gravity lens" chip).
6. The 3D Earth cracking and collapsing as the black hole sinks to the centre.
7. The Sun on its orbit turns into a 6 km black hole, and Earth's orbit stays the same.
8. A top-down city map showing 6 km, then the dark, frozen Earth.
9. The Milky Way with Gaia BH1 about 1,560 light-years from the Sun.
10. The real Earth with the question mark and the subscribe button.

## Facts (as narrated)

**Size and mass**
- The Schwarzschild radius is r = 2GM/c².
- For Earth's mass (5.97×10²⁴ kg), r ≈ 8.87 mm, so the black hole is about 1.77 cm across: the size of a grape.

**Gravity at 1 km**
- g = GM/r² = 3.986×10¹⁴ / (1,000 m)² ≈ 3.99×10⁸ m/s².
- That is about 40.6 million times Earth's surface gravity (9.81 m/s²). Narrated as "40 million times".

**Matter falling in**
- Infalling gas and debris form an accretion disk.
- It heats up through friction and compression and glows. This is how real black holes are seen.

**Light bending**
- Gravitational lensing (general relativity): light passing close to the black hole bends, so the background looks distorted.
- Close to the black hole it forms an Einstein ring.

**Earth's fate**
- A black hole of Earth's mass inside Earth would sink to the centre and swallow the planet.
- The scene is shortened for the Short. The real time scale depends on the details.

**The Sun as a black hole**
- Gravity outside a spherical mass depends only on the mass (the shell theorem / Birkhoff's theorem).
- So a black hole of the Sun's mass would keep Earth on the same orbit.
- Its Schwarzschild radius would be about 2.95 km, so about 5.9 km across: narrated as "6 km".
- With no sunlight, Earth would freeze in the dark.

**Nearest known black hole**
- Gaia BH1 (found in 2022 with ESA's Gaia data) is about 1,560 light-years away, in Ophiuchus.
- It is the nearest confirmed black hole.

**Note**
- The scenario is hypothetical.
- No known process makes a black hole this small today.
- Hawking radiation is negligible at this mass: the evaporation time is far longer than the age of the universe.
