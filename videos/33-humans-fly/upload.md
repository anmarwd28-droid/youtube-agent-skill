## Facts (as narrated)

- **Wingspan of about 7 m:** weight grows with the cube of size and wing area with the square, so a 70–80 kg flyer needs very large wings. The usual estimate for an adult is a wingspan of at least 6.7 m (Yale Scientific, "Could humans fly?", 2013). A minibus such as the Toyota Coaster is about 7 m long.
- **Flight muscles are a fifth of body weight:** in birds the flight muscles (pectoralis and supracoracoideus) average about 17% of body mass and reach 25% or more in strong flyers (Greenewalt 1962; Hartman 1961). The human pectoralis major is about 0.3 kg a side, under 2% of body mass.
- **1,500 kcal per hour:** metabolic power of bird flapping flight scales as P ≈ 57.3 × M^0.813 W (Bishop & Butler). For M = 70 kg: 70^0.813 ≈ 31.6, so P ≈ 1,810 W. 1,810 W × 3,600 s = 6.5 MJ ≈ 1,560 kcal. This extends the bird equation to a human-sized flyer, so it is an estimate. Two full meals are about 750 kcal each.
- **6.5 °C colder per kilometre:** the standard-atmosphere lapse rate (ICAO) is 6.5 °C per km in the troposphere: 20 °C on the ground gives 0.5 °C at 3 km. Air pressure, and with it the oxygen available, is about 70% of sea level at 3 km (70.1 kPa of 101.3 kPa).
- **Empty streets, doors on the roofs, traffic lights in the sky, storms grounding a city:** "what if" consequences, not measured facts. Birds do avoid flying in thunderstorms.
- **Humans did fly on muscle power:** the MIT Daedalus 88, a pedal-powered aircraft with a 34 m wingspan and an empty weight of about 31 kg, flew 115.11 km from Crete to Santorini on 23 April 1988 in 3 h 54 min, pilot Kanellos Kanellopoulos. It still holds the FAI distance and duration records for human-powered aircraft.
