# ماذا لو اختفى كل الزجاج في العالم 5 ثوانٍ؟ — Short #23

50 s, 1080x1920, 30 fps. Global framing.

What it shows, in order:
1. A city street. The shop windows' glass sparkles and vanishes. A "5 s" timer.
2. Inside a flat: the window glass vanishes, curtains billow inward, papers fly off the desk, and wind streaks pour in.
3. The top floor of a skyscraper above a city (seen from about 300 m). A man leaning on the glass wall pulls back when it vanishes.
4. A car on a motorway: the windscreen vanishes, and wind hits the driver's face and hair.
5. The 3D night Earth with glowing undersea internet cables across the Atlantic and to Africa, Europe and India.
6. A cable cut open on the seabed: armour wires, copper, and hair-thin glass fibres pulsing with light.
7. The cables go dark: calls ✕, streaming ✕, payments ✕, and a 5-second countdown.
8. The surprise: a pair of eyeglasses stays, because the lenses are plastic.
9. Earth with the question mark and the subscribe button.

## Facts (as narrated)

- **The scenario:** every glass object vanishes for 5 seconds, then returns. Plastics, acrylic and metal stay.
- **Skyscrapers:** many have floor-to-ceiling glass curtain walls. Supertall towers rise hundreds of metres; the scene shows about 300 m.
- **Car windscreens** are laminated safety glass.
- **The internet:**
  - Undersea fibre-optic cables carry about 99% of international (intercontinental) data traffic (TeleGeography; widely cited by ITU and others).
  - Optical fibres are made of ultra-pure silica glass about as thin as a hair (about 125 µm with cladding).
  - Data travels as pulses of light.
  - With the fibres gone for 5 seconds, intercontinental links would drop: calls, streaming and payments.
- **Eyeglasses:** most spectacle lenses sold today are plastic (CR-39, polycarbonate, high-index plastics), not glass. In the US and many other markets, plastic is the large majority of lenses sold. So most glasses would stay.
