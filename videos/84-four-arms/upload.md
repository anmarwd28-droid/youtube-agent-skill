## Facts (numbers and sources)
- One arm (upper arm 2.8% + forearm 1.6% + hand 0.6%) is about 5% of body mass (Dempster 1955 segment data, as tabulated in D. A. Winter, "Biomechanics and Motor Control of Human Movement", 4th ed., 2009, Table 4.1). Two extra arms ≈ +10% body mass: 70 kg → about 77 kg on the scale.
- Moving a limb needs its own area in the primary motor cortex (precentral gyrus), which holds a body map where the hands already take a large share (Penfield & Boldrey 1937; Penfield & Rasmussen 1950). Two new hands would need new or rewired motor areas; the motor and sensory maps are known to reorganise after limb changes (e.g. amputation studies, Flor et al., Nature 1995).
- Extra muscle is extra living tissue to feed: skeletal muscle uses about 13 kcal per kg per day at rest and far more when working (Elia 1992; Wang et al., Am J Clin Nutr 2010), so energy needs rise, and the heart must pump more blood to supply it (cardiac output follows the body's oxygen demand).
- Clothing, car seats and seat belts are designed around a two-armed body (sleeves, the diagonal shoulder belt of a three-point belt).
- In front-crawl swimming the arms give most of the propulsion, roughly 85–90% in sprint studies (Deschodt, Arsac & Rouard, Eur J Appl Physiol 1999; Hollander et al. 1988). Extra arms would add push (and also drag), so new records are a possibility, said with «قد».
- No land vertebrate (tetrapod) has more than four limbs; some lost them (snakes, caecilians). All tetrapods descend from lobe-finned fish of the Devonian with two pairs of fins; transitional forms like Tiktaalik date to about 375 million years ago and early four-limbed tetrapods (Acanthostega, Ichthyostega) to about 365 million years ago (Daeschler, Shubin & Jenkins, Nature 2006; Clack, "Gaining Ground", 2012). «نحو ٣٧٠ مليون سنة» is a rounded figure for that transition.
- Bird and bat wings are modified forelimbs with the same bones as the human arm: humerus, radius and ulna, wrist and finger bones (classic homology; any vertebrate zoology text).
- Insects have six legs (three pairs on the thorax; the class name Hexapoda means "six feet").
- People born with extra limbs (polymelia) are extremely rare, a developmental condition, not a new body plan (not in the narration).
- The narration is global; the street, the kitchen and the shop stand for any city in the world.
