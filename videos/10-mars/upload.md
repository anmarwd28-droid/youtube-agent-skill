## Facts (approximate, as narrated)
- Earth→Mars transfer trips take about 6–9 months; ~7 months is typical.
- Mars surface gravity is 3.71 m/s², about 38% of Earth's (less than half). A 70 kg person would weigh like ~27 kg.
- With the same push-off speed, jump height and air time scale with 1/g: ~2.6× higher (≈0.5 m on Earth → ≈1.3 m on Mars), ignoring the suit's mass.
- The atmosphere is ~95% CO₂, ~2.8% nitrogen, ~2% argon, ~0.17% oxygen; mean surface pressure ≈0.6 kPa, under 1% of Earth's.
- At that pressure an unprotected person loses consciousness in roughly 10–15 seconds.
- Mean surface temperature is about −60 °C (≈ −63 °C).
- The daytime sky is a dusty butterscotch/pink; around the setting Sun it turns blue (fine dust scatters blue light forward), as photographed by NASA rovers.
- Dust storms can grow to cover the whole planet and last weeks to months (e.g. 2001, 2018).
- Mars has no global magnetic field (only weak crustal remnants) and a thin atmosphere, so surface radiation is far higher than on Earth; a few metres of regolith or lava tubes would shield habitats.
- Water ice is buried under thin soil in many mid- and high-latitude regions (seen by orbiters and exposed in fresh craters).
- A Martian solar day (sol) is 24 h 39 min 35 s; a Martian year is 687 Earth days (≈1.88 years).
- Sizes and distances in the space shots are not to scale (shown on screen).
