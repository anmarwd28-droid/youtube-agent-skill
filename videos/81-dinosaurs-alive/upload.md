## Facts (numbers and sources)
- The Chicxulub impact, about 66 million years ago, by an asteroid roughly 10 km (10–15 km) across, ended the non-avian dinosaurs (Schulte et al. 2010, Science).
- The largest sauropods (e.g. Argentinosaurus, Patagotitan) were about 30–37 m long and weighed roughly 60–80 tonnes; an African elephant is about 6 tonnes, so about ten elephants.
- A large sauropod needed on the order of hundreds of kilograms of plant food a day (energy-budget estimates).
- T. rex had one of the strongest bites known for a land animal: about 35,000 N, up to ~57,000 N at the back teeth (Bates & Falkingham 2012); fossils and coprolites show it crushed bone. Human ≈ 1,300 N max; saltwater crocodile ≈ 16,000 N (Erickson et al. 2012).
- T. rex top speed is estimated at about 20 km/h (Sellers et al. 2017); fast human runners exceed 25–30 km/h (Usain Bolt peaked near 44 km/h).
- Most Mesozoic mammals were small; mammals diversified and grew larger after the dinosaurs died out.
- Birds are living theropod dinosaurs; collagen sequences from T. rex group with birds, closest to the chicken and ostrich (Asara et al. 2007; Organ et al. 2008).
