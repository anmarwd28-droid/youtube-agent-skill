## Facts (as narrated)
Scenario: suddenly nothing that runs on electricity or fuel works (engines, power, electronics). Buildings, clothes, books and simple tools stay.
- **Satellites (visual only):** more than 10,000 active satellites orbit the Earth (2025); without power and control they keep orbiting but go dead.
- **Billions of devices:** about 5.5 billion people use the internet and there are more than 7 billion smartphones in use (ITU, GSMA). All stop at once.
- **Cars, ships and planes:** they all need engines that burn fuel and electronics to run; ships with no engine drift with wind and current.
- **Taps run dry within days:** most city water systems lift and pressurise water with electric pumps; water towers and elevated tanks hold only hours to about a day of supply. Narrated as "خلال أيام".
- **Shops empty in about three days:** supermarkets work "just in time" and usually hold about three days of stock; without trucks nothing is restocked.
- **About half of humanity eats thanks to factory fertiliser:** synthetic nitrogen fertiliser (Haber–Bosch process) supports about 48% of the world's population (Erisman et al., Nature Geoscience 2008; Smil: 40–50%). 8.2 billion × ~0.48 ≈ 4 billion, shown as «نحوُ ٤ مليارات إنسان».
- **Hospitals:** monitors, ventilators, dialysis, labs and medicine factories all need power; no new medicines are made.
- **About one billion 200 years ago:** the world population passed one billion around 1804 and was about 1.0–1.1 billion around 1820 (UN; Our World in Data), fed by farming with muscle power, animals and no synthetic fertiliser.
- **Today more than eight billion:** about 8.2 billion (UN World Population Prospects 2024).
- **The twist, stone tools more than 3 million years old:** the oldest known stone tools, Lomekwi 3 in West Turkana, Kenya, are about 3.3 million years old (Harmand et al., Nature 2015). Technology is older than our species.
- **Timeline marks:** controlled fire about 1 million years ago (Wonderwerk Cave, South Africa; Berna et al., PNAS 2012); the wheel about 5,500 years ago (Mesopotamia); machines about 200 years ago (the steam engines of the Industrial Revolution).
