## Facts (numbers and sources)
- An adult skeleton usually has 206 bones (Gray's Anatomy; the number varies a little between people).
- Skeletal muscles move the body only by pulling on bones through tendons; without a rigid lever a contracting muscle just shortens and bulges.
- World population ≈ 8.2 billion (UN, 2025), "eight billion".
- The skull protects the brain; the ribcage keeps the chest wall rigid so the diaphragm can draw air in (an unstable chest wall, as in flail chest, makes breathing very hard).
- The three ear ossicles (malleus, incus, stapes) are the smallest bones in the body (the stapes is about 3 mm); without them sound conduction falls by roughly 50–60 dB, so the world sounds almost silent.
- Red bone marrow makes about 2 million red blood cells every second (≈ 2–3 million/s; e.g. Higgins 2015, Sender & Milo 2021).
- A newborn has about 270–300 bones or bony parts; many fuse during growth (skull plates, the sacrum, the pelvis) to give 206 in adults.
