## Facts (as narrated)
- **About 700 years ago, a third to a half of Europe:** the Black Death reached Europe in 1347 (through Caffa in Crimea and then Mediterranean ports) and swept it until 1351. It killed an estimated 30–50% (some estimates up to 60%) of Europe's population, about 25–50 million people, and millions more in Asia, the Middle East and North Africa.
- **A small bacterium carried by fleas from rats to people:** plague is caused by Yersinia pestis, a rod-shaped bacterium (it stains darker at both ends). It lives in wild rodents and is passed mostly by the bite of infected fleas, classically the rat flea Xenopsylla cheopis.
- **Its worst form hits the lungs and spreads by coughing:** pneumonic plague infects the lungs and is the only form that passes directly from person to person, through respiratory droplets. Untreated, it is almost always fatal.
- **Years with caravans and ships, one day by plane:** the 14th-century pandemic moved along trade routes over several years (Central Asia in the 1330s–40s, Crimea 1346, Europe 1347–1351). Today a flight reaches almost any city in under 24 hours.
- **Antibiotics: with early treatment about nine in ten survive:** plague is treated with common antibiotics (streptomycin, gentamicin, doxycycline, ciprofloxacin). With prompt treatment the fatality rate falls to about 10% or less (WHO, CDC); untreated bubonic plague kills 30–60%.
- **Quarantine and rodent control:** isolating patients, treating their contacts with antibiotics, and controlling fleas and rodents stop outbreaks (as in Madagascar in 2017). The word "quarantine" comes from the 40 days (quaranta giorni) ships waited in 14th–15th-century ports because of the plague.
- **Plague never went away: hundreds of cases a year:** WHO recorded 3,248 cases and 584 deaths worldwide from 2010 to 2015. Most cases today are in Madagascar, the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Peru, with occasional cases in Mongolia, China and the western United States.
- **Global framing:** the street and the people stand for any city in the world.
