## Facts (numbers and sources)
- Jumping spiders cling with tufts of hairs (scopulae) at their leg tips; each hair splits into many tiny setules that stick by van der Waals forces, a weak attraction between molecules. The spider Evarcha arcuata can hold about 170 times its own body weight (Kesel et al., Smart Materials and Structures 2004).
- A jumping spider weighs about 15 mg; a 70 kg person is about 4.7 million times heavier.
- Square-cube law: doubling every length makes the area 4 times larger and the volume (weight) 8 times larger.
- For a human to climb like a gecko, sticky pads would have to cover about 40 % of the body surface (about 80 % of the front), or feet of European shoe size 145 (Labonte et al., PNAS 2016). Size 145 in Paris points (2/3 cm each) is about 97 cm.
- Dirt and water films reduce dry adhesion; geckos keep their toes clean by self-cleaning as they walk (Hansen & Autumn, PNAS 2005; Stark et al. 2012 on wet surfaces).
- In 2014 Stanford engineers (Elliot Hawkes, Mark Cutkosky) made hand-held gecko-inspired adhesive pads, and a 70 kg person used them to climb a glass wall about 3.6 m high (Journal of the Royal Society Interface, 2014).
