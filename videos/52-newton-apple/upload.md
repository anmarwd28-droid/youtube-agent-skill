The science angle: in 1666 the plague sends young Newton home; an apple falls and he asks why the Moon doesn't fall too; the answer: the Moon does fall, about 1.3 mm every second, but it moves sideways fast enough to keep missing. Without that idea the law of gravity might have come decades later, and with it: Halley's comet predicted (1758), Neptune found on paper (1846), Newton's cannonball at about 8 km/s (orbits, satellites, maps, weather, live TV), Apollo 8 ("Newton is doing most of the driving"). The twist: the apple never hit his head (he only saw it fall), and the tree is still alive more than 350 years later.

## Facts (as narrated)
- **1666, the plague:** Cambridge University closed in 1665–1667 because of the Great Plague; Newton (born 1642) went home to Woolsthorpe Manor, his mother's farm in Lincolnshire, where he worked on gravity, optics and calculus (the "annus mirabilis").
- **The apple:** William Stukeley, *Memoirs of Sir Isaac Newton's Life* (written 1752, from a conversation with Newton in 1726): the notion of gravitation came to him "occasioned by the fall of an apple, as he sat in a contemplative mood"; he asked why it always falls straight down to the ground. John Conduitt's account says the same. Nothing says it hit his head; that detail came later in popular retellings.
- **The Moon falls 1.3 mm a second:** the Moon's centripetal acceleration a = 4π²r/T² with r = 384,400 km and T = 27.32 days (2.36 × 10⁶ s) is 0.0027 m/s²; in one second it "falls" ½at² ≈ 1.36 mm below its straight path. This equals g/3,600 (the Moon is about 60 Earth radii away, and gravity falls with the square of distance: 9.81/3,600 = 0.0027). Its speed along the orbit is about 1.02 km/s.
- **The law:** Newton published the law of universal gravitation in the *Principia* (1687): F = G·m₁m₂/r².
- **"Maybe decades later":** speculation, said as "ربّما". Robert Hooke and others had guessed an inverse-square pull (Hooke's letter to Newton, 1679), but nobody else derived orbits from it; Leibniz developed calculus independently.
- **Halley's comet:** Edmond Halley used Newton's laws to show the comets of 1531, 1607 and 1682 were one object and predicted its return in 1758; it was first seen again on 25 December 1758 (Halley died in 1742).
- **Neptune:** Urbain Le Verrier calculated from the pull on Uranus's orbit where an unknown planet must be; Johann Galle found Neptune on 23 September 1846, within about 1° of the prediction (John Couch Adams made a similar calculation).
- **Newton's cannon:** in *A Treatise of the System of the World* Newton imagined a cannon on a high mountain: faster shots land farther away, and fast enough the ball goes round the Earth. The circular orbital speed near the surface is √(GM/R) ≈ 7.9 km/s ("about 8 km per second", ignoring air).
- **Satellites:** GPS and phone maps, weather satellites and satellite TV broadcasts all depend on orbits worked out with Newton's laws.
- **Apollo 8 (December 1968), the first humans to travel to the Moon:** when ground control said Bill Anders's son asked who was driving, Anders answered: "I think Isaac Newton is doing most of the driving right now." The ship flew a free-return path round the Moon.
- **The tree:** the original apple tree still grows at Woolsthorpe Manor (National Trust). It fell in a storm around 1820 but re-rooted and kept growing; it is well over 350 years old.
