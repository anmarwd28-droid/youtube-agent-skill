## Facts (numbers and sources)
- Time dilation (special and general relativity): moving clocks run slow; tested with atomic clocks on planes (Hafele–Keating 1971) and corrected for every day in GPS.
- Cosmonaut Sergei Krikalev spent about 803 days in orbit; his net time dilation makes him about 0.02 s (1/50 s) "younger" than if he had stayed on Earth («أكثرَ من ثمانمئةِ يومٍ», «جزءٍ من خمسينَ من الثانية»).
- Wormholes (Einstein–Rosen bridges, 1935) are solutions of general relativity; a traversable wormhole needs "exotic" matter with negative energy density to stay open (Morris & Thorne, Am. J. Phys. 1988). Negative energy exists only in tiny amounts (Casimir effect), far too little.
- A wormhole could be turned into a time machine by moving one mouth (Morris, Thorne & Yurtsever, PRL 1988), but such a machine can only take you back to the moment it was made into a time machine, never earlier.
- The grandfather paradox: changing the past could stop your own birth.
- Hawking's chronology protection conjecture (Phys. Rev. D 1992): quantum effects would build up and destroy a time machine as it forms.
- Hawking's party for time travellers: held on 28 June 2009 at Cambridge; the invitations were made public only after the party; nobody came (shown in *Stephen Hawking's Universe*/Discovery, 2010; he described it in 2012).
- The Great Pyramid of Giza was built about 4,500 years ago (about 2560 BCE); sauropod dinosaurs lived about 200 to 66 million years ago.
