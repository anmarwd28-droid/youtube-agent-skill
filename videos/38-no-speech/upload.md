8. Two people signing on the street; three cards with hand shapes.
9. A classroom: deaf children signing in a circle; chalk hand shapes and «لغةٌ جديدة» on the board.

## Facts (as narrated)

- **About 16,000 words a day.** Mehl et al., "Are women really more talkative than men?", Science 317 (2007): students spoke about 16,000 words a day on average, women and men alike.
- **About 7,000 languages.** Ethnologue (2024) lists about 7,160 living languages.
- **Phones, radio and songs need the voice.** Calls, broadcasts and sung lyrics are speech; instrumental music would remain.
- **Emergencies.** A spoken warning and voice calls to emergency services would be impossible (text-to-emergency services exist in some countries, e.g. 112 SMS in the EU, text-to-911 in parts of the US).
- **Writing and messages** already carry much of daily communication.
- **Facial expressions.** Basic emotions such as happiness, fear and anger are recognised across most cultures (Ekman & Friesen 1971 and later cross-cultural studies; the degree of universality is debated, hence «معظم الشعوب»).
- **More than 300 sign languages, about 70 million deaf people.** World Federation of the Deaf: about 70 million deaf people use more than 300 different sign languages.
- **Nicaraguan Sign Language.** When new schools for deaf children opened in Managua (1977 and 1980), the children, who had no shared sign language, created one; younger children made it a full language within a few years (Kegl, Senghas & Coppola 1999; Senghas & Coppola, Psychological Science 2001).
- **Touch in the dark.** Deafblind people use tactile signing: the listener rests their hands on the signer's hands to feel the signs.
- **Global framing:** the street and the people stand for anyone in the world.
