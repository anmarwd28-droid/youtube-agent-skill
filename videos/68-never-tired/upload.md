## Facts (numbers and sources)
- Marathon world record: 2:00:35, Kelvin Kiptum, Chicago 2023 («نحوُ ساعتين»). 200 m world-record pace (Usain Bolt, 19.19 s ≈ 37.5 km/h) over 42.195 km gives about 67.5 minutes («نحوُ ساعةٍ وعشرِ دقائق»); a sprinter's short-race speed held for the whole distance, if fatigue did not exist.
- Fatigue is protective: the brain slows the muscles down before damage (the "central governor" idea, Noakes 2012; muscle strain injuries rise with fatigue). Core body temperature above about 40 °C is heat stroke, a medical emergency.
- Muscle and liver glycogen (about 400–500 g, roughly 2,000 kcal) fuel about 90–120 minutes of hard exercise; when it runs out, marathon runners "hit the wall" («نحوَ ساعتينِ فقط»).
- WHO/ILO joint estimate (Environment International, 2021): working 55 hours or more a week led to 745,000 deaths from stroke and ischaemic heart disease in 2016 («سبعمئةٍ وخمسةٍ وأربعينَ ألفَ إنسانٍ في عامٍ واحد»).
- Heart muscle is highly resistant to fatigue (it is full of mitochondria and runs on a steady oxygen supply). At about 70–80 beats a minute (about 100,000 a day) a heart beats about 2.5–3 billion times in a lifetime («نحوَ ثلاثةِ ملياراتِ مرّةٍ»). It never stops; it only relaxes for a fraction of a second between beats.
