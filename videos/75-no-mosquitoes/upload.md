## Facts (numbers and sources)
- Malaria killed about 597,000 people in 2023, about three quarters of them African children under five (WHO World Malaria Report 2024). Dengue, Zika, yellow fever and West Nile are also spread by mosquitoes, which makes the mosquito the animal linked to the most human deaths.
- Other animals per year: snakebites 81,000–138,000 deaths (WHO), rabies about 59,000 (mostly from dogs, WHO), sharks about 5–10 unprovoked fatal attacks (International Shark Attack File).
- About 3,500 species of mosquitoes are known; only a few hundred bite or bother humans (Fang, "A world without mosquitoes", Nature 466, 2010).
- Only females bite: they need protein from blood to make eggs; males feed on nectar and plant juices.
- Larvae live in still water, hang under the surface and breathe through a siphon; they are eaten by fish, tadpoles, dragonfly nymphs and other animals; frogs eat adults.
- In the Arctic summer, huge mosquito swarms over the tundra ponds are food for migrating birds; researchers estimated insect-eating bird numbers there could drop by more than half without them (Nature 2010).
- Some plants are pollinated by mosquitoes, such as the blunt-leaf orchid (Platanthera obtusata) of the north, whose pollen packets stick to the mosquito's eyes.
- Most ecologists quoted in Nature 2010 thought other insects would fill most of the gap.
- Scientists now target only dangerous species, e.g. gene-drive work on Anopheles gambiae (Target Malaria) and Wolbachia mosquitoes against dengue (World Mosquito Program).
