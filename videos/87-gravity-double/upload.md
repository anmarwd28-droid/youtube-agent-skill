## Facts (numbers and sources)
- The scenario: the Earth's surface gravity g doubles (9.8 → 19.6 m/s²) for everyone at once, e.g. the Earth's pull doubling at the same radius. Your mass stays 70 kg; your weight W = m·g doubles, so a scale reads 140 kg (a scale measures force and is calibrated for 1 g). Basic Newtonian mechanics (e.g. OpenStax University Physics vol. 1, ch. 5–6).
- Heart and brain: the heart must push blood up a column of about 30–40 cm to the brain; the hydrostatic pressure drop ρgh doubles with g, so less pressure reaches the brain. Standing up fast lets blood pool in the legs (orthostatic effect), and under higher g this causes grey-out or fainting (G-LOC). Unprotected people typically grey out around +3 to +5 g; pilots tolerate more only briefly with G-suits and straining. Sustained 2 g is very tiring (centrifuge studies). Sources: NASA/FAA aerospace physiology (FAA "Acceleration in Aviation: G-Force", AM-400-09/3); Whinnery & Forster, "The +Gz-induced loss of consciousness curve", Extreme Physiology & Medicine 2013. Narration says «قد يُغمى عليك» (may faint).
- Falling from the same height: impact energy = m·g·h doubles; impact speed v = √(2gh) is √2 ≈ 1.41 times higher; the fall time t = √(2h/g) is 1/√2 ≈ 0.71 times (on screen: 1.0 s vs 0.71 s from 5 m, energy ×1 vs ×2).
- A thrown ball: range R = v²·sin 2θ / g, so with the same throw speed and angle it goes half as far (and half as high) (drag ignored).
- Structures: buildings and bridges carry their own weight (dead load) plus traffic; doubling g doubles every load. Structures are designed with safety factors on loads of roughly 1.2–1.6 (load factors in ASCE 7 / Eurocode EN 1990) plus material factors, so many modern ones would hold but some, especially old masonry, would crack and some would fail. Narration: «قد يتشقّقُ بعضُها أو ينهار» (some may crack or collapse).
- Planes: lift L = ½ρv²SC_L must equal the weight; with double weight a plane needs √2 ≈ 1.41 times the take-off speed (same air density and wing), so longer runways and more thrust. Birds face the same problem (minimum flight power grows with weight^1.5).
- Rockets: with the Earth's pull doubled at the same radius, the speed for a low orbit rises from about 7.9 to about 11.2 km/s (×√2, v = √(GM/r)) and the escape speed from 11.2 to about 15.8 km/s; gravity losses also grow. By the rocket equation the fuel mass grows exponentially with the needed Δv, so far more fuel is needed and most of today's rockets could not reach orbit. Narration: «وقوداً أكثرَ بكثير» (no exact number).
- Air: surface pressure = weight of the air column per area = M_air·g/A, so with the same amount of air the pressure at the ground doubles (about 2 atmospheres), and the scale height H = kT/(mg) halves: the air is pressed closer to the ground (denser near the surface). Standard atmospheric physics (e.g. Wallace & Hobbs, Atmospheric Science).
- Trees: the maximum height of a tree is limited by lifting water against gravity and by buckling under its own weight (Greenhill: critical height ∝ (E/ρg)^(1/3)); with double g, plants would over time grow shorter (Koch et al., "The limits to tree height", Nature 2004; Niklas, Plant Biomechanics). Narration: «تنمو الأشجارُ مع الزمنِ أقصر».
- The Moon: if the Earth's pull doubles (μ = GM doubles), the Moon's present speed (≈1.02 km/s) is only 1/√2 of the new circular speed at its distance, so it falls onto an elliptical orbit with today's distance as its farthest point. From the vis-viva equation: semi-major axis a = 2r₀/3, closest point r_p = 2a − r₀ = r₀/3 ≈ 128,000 km (a third of 384,400 km). The tidal pull of the Moon grows with 1/r³, so at the closest point the tides are many times higher (×27 in tidal force at a third of the distance; still far outside the Roche limit). The diagram is not to scale (the Earth and the orbit are drawn closer so they fit the screen).
- Roller coasters: at the bottom of a big drop riders feel about 3 to 4.5 g for a second or two (positive g, e.g. typical large steel coasters ~3.5–4.5 g; ride design limits in ASTM F2291). Narration: «تشعرُ بأكثرَ من ضعفِ وزنِك، لكن لثوانٍ فقط».
