## Facts (approximate, as narrated)

**The volcano and its magma**
- Yellowstone (Wyoming, USA) sits on a volcanic system that produced three caldera-forming eruptions: about 2.1 million years ago (Huckleberry Ridge, about 2,500 km³), 1.3 million years ago (Mesa Falls, about 280 km³) and about 640,000 years ago (Lava Creek, about 1,000 km³; recent dating puts it near 631,000).
- 1,000 km³ is a cube 10 km on each side. Mount Everest is 8.85 km high.
- Seismic imaging shows two reservoirs under the caldera:
  - an upper rhyolitic one at about 5–17 km depth
  - a lower basaltic one at about 20–50 km
- The upper reservoir is mostly crystalline mush. Only roughly 5–15% is melt (some newer estimates go up to about 20%), so it is not "a lake of lava".

**What a new super-eruption would do**
- The column of a super-eruption would rise well over 30 km, into the stratosphere. Airliners cruise at about 10–12 km.
- USGS modelling (Mastin et al., 2014): more than a metre of ash near the park, centimetres in the US Midwest, millimetres on both coasts.
- Volcanic ash is pulverised rock and volcanic glass: hard and abrasive.
  - It damages jet engines. For scale, Eyjafjallajökull in 2010, a far smaller eruption, shut much of European airspace for days.
  - It ruins crops and its weight can collapse roofs.
- Sulphur gases form sulphate aerosols in the stratosphere. These spread around the globe and reflect sunlight, cooling the climate for a few years. For scale, Pinatubo in 1991 cooled the globe by about 0.5 °C.

**A smaller real example**
- Tambora (Indonesia) erupted in April 1815. 1816 became the "Year Without a Summer" in Europe and North America, with June snow and widespread crop failures.

**The good news**
- The USGS estimates the yearly chance of another caldera-forming eruption at about 1 in 730,000, which is below 1 in 700,000.
- The Yellowstone Volcano Observatory monitors earthquakes, GPS ground motion and gases continuously.
- The most likely future events are hydrothermal explosions or lava flows, not a super-eruption.
